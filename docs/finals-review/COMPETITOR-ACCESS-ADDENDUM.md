## Additional published material

ARC's indexed README describes an optional model-based language layer that
bypasses official templates, proposes structured preferences, and checks proposed
evidence against catalog phrases. It reports separate language diagnostics,
including .955602 for natural wording with verbatim attributes and .759952 when
attributes themselves are paraphrased. It also reports substantial tail latency
when recovery scans the full catalog. These are the author's measurements with
a model-generated shopper, not comparable scores on our language suites. This
is a documented extension for broader language, not evidence of a concealed
model in the .9804 official-template result. Our punctuation/provenance repair
does not establish equivalent semantic-language coverage.
Its rank-one arrival audit concerns its chosen ranker; that does not establish
an information-theoretic lower bound for every possible ordering or policy.
[Source: indexed ARC README](https://github.com/kelvin715/techjam-2026-shopping-copilot).

The indexed `evidence.py` estimates clarification value by grouping plausible
candidates by predicted replies. It treats a non-answer differently from a
positive disclosure and subtracts a turn cost. Its diagnostic explanation
routine searches removals of constraints and reranks to find a change in the
first recommendation. This supports the published emphasis on answerability
and inspectable decisions. It does not expose the complete ranking/runtime
implementation, and the question model's assumptions are not an end-to-end
optimality proof. We retain our full-transcript coverage and actual-visible-text
reply grouping; their explanation interface remains a useful presentation idea.
[Source: indexed ARC evidence module](https://github.com/kelvin715/techjam-2026-shopping-copilot/blob/main/src/evidence.py).

Fable7's indexed README describes a 16-feature pairwise linear ranker followed
by global ordered-card lookup, popularity ordering, and rank-one exposure while
the prefix remains ambiguous. It identifies a separate, unmerged parser branch
with broader wording coverage and says the TinyBERT component was removed from
main. These details reinforce that its .978 result is mostly deterministic
retrieval/state/output engineering. The stated broader parser work is relevant
to our language repair, but its unpublished-to-us implementation has not been
reproduced or transplanted.
[Source: indexed Fable7 README](https://github.com/SrivathsanRam/tiktok-techjam-conversational-search).
